## Commercial alternative license

A commercial alternative is optional and applies only to material expressly
identified in a written agreement signed by the licensor and customer. Before
including any file, feature, or contribution, the licensor must own the relevant
rights or hold explicit written authority from the actual rights holder to
grant the specified commercial rights. Repository inclusion, authorship
metadata, attribution, pull-request acceptance, merge, or publication under
AGPL does not by itself prove that authority.

Absent that documented authority, the material is not included in the
commercial license. It may remain available under AGPL if the rights holder
has granted that public license. Third-party dependencies, copied benchmark
code, fixtures, parent-project code, and other externally licensed materials
remain governed by their own terms unless the relevant rights holder separately
grants additional rights.

## Contributions and commercial permission

Contributors retain their rights. Contributions accepted into the public
project must carry an explicit AGPL grant for the rights needed to publish and
maintain them under AGPL. A contributor's separate permission for commercial
relicensing is an affirmative, optional authorization, independent of the AGPL
grant. The project must not treat a contribution as commercially sublicensable
unless the actual rights holder has provided written permission covering that
scope. If the rights holder is an employer, client, or other entity, permission
must come from a person authorized to bind that rights holder.

The repository's [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) describes the proposed
intake policy. It is not itself a signed contributor agreement and does not
retroactively authorize commercial relicensing of existing contributions.
Until explicit commercial permission is recorded, the project must exclude that
contribution from any commercial alternative license.

## Feature revenue sharing

For an official commercial product operated by the licensor or project, the
policy intent is to allocate 100% of revenue attributable to a specifically
named feature to that feature's contributor group. This is a project-to-
contributors arrangement under a separate written agreement; it is not a fee or
royalty owed by AGPL users or independent forks.

The default operational design is a low-cost, auditable virtual subledger that
records the feature-level amount payable and its status. The subledger is an
accounting record only: it is not represented as a bank escrow, trust, custody
account, or segregated legal asset. The project does not receive or hold the
money merely by recording the payable. The signed agreement must identify the
commercial payer or payment provider responsible for the funds and, where
available and lawful, provide for direct payment to contributors or a recipient
arrangement they designate. The licensor/project should not receive funds for
later personal redistribution by default.

The contributor group negotiates its internal shares. Until the group has a
valid written allocation agreement or a final outcome under the dispute
procedure they accepted in advance, the project does not decide individual
shares, withdraw funds, or distribute the disputed amount. The signed agreement
must specify the negotiation period, a neutral mediation process, and the
independent adjudication or arbitration process if mediation does not resolve
the dispute. The project will not impose code-line, commit-count, or PR-weight
formulas after a dispute arises. Once a valid allocation and payment instruction
are received, the named payer or payment provider pays contributors directly
where the signed arrangement and applicable law permit.

A feature-revenue agreement must define the feature boundary and attribution
basis, revenue pool and any permitted deductions, payer/payment provider, ledger
and reporting, payment instructions, cost and interest treatment, tax reporting
and withholding responsibility, refunds and failed payments, dispute process,
and governing law. The repository template does not establish that a bank,
notary, regulated custodian, direct-payment provider, tax treatment, or
particular dispute forum is available or legally suitable. These matters must
be selected and reviewed by qualified local legal and tax advisers before any
payment arrangement is used.

Feature participation and commercial-relicensing permission are independent.
A contributor's agreement to participate in this revenue arrangement does not
authorize commercial sublicensing of that contributor's code. The arrangement
applies only to the official product, feature, payer, and parties named in an
executed agreement. Independent forks are entirely outside it, and it never
conditions, limits, or adds a fee to anyone's exercise of AGPL rights.

The non-executed starting form is [`FEATURE-REVENUE-AGREEMENT.md`](FEATURE-REVENUE-AGREEMENT.md).
No payment obligation or custody relationship is created until the relevant
parties execute a complete agreement.
